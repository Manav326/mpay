# mPay Legal & Policy Checklist

Date: 2026-09-27
Status: Target checklist; legal wording requires qualified review

## 1. Purpose

This checklist maps the intended mPay business model to the documents and disclosures that must exist before production.

Canonical business description:

> mPay is intended to operate as a customer-facing service platform for recharge and chauffeur-driven rental services, using PayU/other appropriately authorised payment infrastructure for customer payments. mPay earns contractual service/platform commissions or fees from the underlying service transactions. Where customers preload funds and maintain a reusable balance for subsequent third-party services or withdrawal, that stored-value functionality will be implemented through an appropriately regulated wallet/payment structure rather than treating the customer's funds as unrestricted mPay operating funds.

## 2. Corporate and provider documents

Required before production:
- incorporated entity documents;
- company bank/current-account evidence;
- PayU merchant agreement/KYC under the incorporated entity;
- Razorpay agreement/KYC if retained;
- confirmation of permitted business/use cases;
- regulated wallet/payment partner agreement, if partner model is selected;
- settlement-account documentation;
- recharge-provider agreement;
- future BBPS participant/partner agreement where applicable;
- vendor agreements for independent vehicle owners.

## 3. Customer documents

The customer-facing legal set should cover:
- Terms of Service;
- Privacy Notice;
- Wallet/Add Money terms;
- recharge terms;
- rental terms;
- cancellation policy;
- refund/reversal policy;
- withdrawal/cash-out terms;
- prohibited-use/fraud terms;
- grievance and escalation mechanism;
- account closure/deletion mechanism;
- applicable fee disclosure.

The exact legal wording must be reviewed against the final provider and regulatory structure.

## 4. Wallet disclosures

The wallet documentation must clearly explain:
- what the balance represents;
- who provides/holds the stored value;
- where legally applicable, who the regulated issuer/provider is;
- permitted uses;
- applicable limits;
- withdrawal rules;
- refund rules;
- expiry, if any;
- fees, if any;
- failed/pending transaction treatment;
- grievance route;
- account closure treatment.

mPay must not describe itself as a bank or deposit-taking institution.

## 5. Recharge disclosures

The customer experience must distinguish:
- payment status;
- recharge request status;
- operator/provider;
- successful recharge;
- failed recharge;
- pending/unknown recharge;
- refund/reversal.

If BBPS is not yet active, customer-facing material must not imply that mPay is currently a BBPS participant.

## 6. Rental disclosures

Rental terms should identify:
- customer-facing contracting party;
- underlying independent vendor/service provider where legally required;
- vehicle and driver responsibilities;
- fare calculation;
- taxes/fees;
- platform/service fee where charged;
- booking confirmation;
- cancellation;
- no-show;
- service failure;
- refund;
- vendor substitution where permitted;
- customer support and grievance process.

## 7. Vendor documents

Vendor onboarding should include:
- identity/business details;
- bank settlement details;
- vehicle documentation;
- driver documentation where applicable;
- insurance/permit/fitness/registration evidence as legally applicable;
- contractual representations;
- safety obligations;
- service standards;
- cancellation/service-failure obligations;
- platform fee/settlement terms;
- tax/invoice requirements;
- fraud and prohibited-use obligations.

## 8. Privacy and data

The product must maintain:
- data inventory;
- purpose mapping;
- consent/notice mapping where applicable;
- retention schedule;
- deletion process;
- access/correction mechanism where applicable;
- processor/vendor register;
- security controls;
- incident response;
- user-facing privacy notice.

## 9. Grievance management

Production should have:
- public grievance contact;
- responsible owner;
- ticket/reference number;
- acknowledgement process;
- resolution timeline;
- escalation path;
- payment-provider escalation path;
- records of complaints and resolution.

## 10. Regulatory wording rule

Do not publish:
- "RBI approved" unless the specific entity/product approval exists;
- "BBPS enabled/partner" without supporting agreement/role evidence;
- "bank wallet" or similar language suggesting a banking relationship;
- claims that customer funds are protected/segregated unless the actual regulated structure provides that protection.

## 11. Final legal review gate

Before production launch, qualified Indian legal/regulatory and tax review should confirm:
1. entity structure;
2. wallet/PPI/payment-system classification;
3. payment-provider role;
4. customer-money custody/settlement;
5. withdrawal mechanism;
6. recharge provider/BBPS role;
7. rental merchant/vendor structure;
8. GST/tax treatment of gross customer consideration and platform fee;
9. consumer protection and cancellation/refund obligations;
10. privacy/data obligations;
11. grievance and dispute responsibilities.

## 12. Evidence

Every final policy/contract should be stored with:
- document name/version;
- effective date;
- approving owner;
- legal reviewer;
- provider counterparty;
- linked technical implementation;
- superseded version where applicable.
